Employment Contract Guidelines

1. At-Will Employment vs. Fixed Term
At-will employment allows either party to terminate without cause, while fixed-term contracts provide job security but limit flexibility. Clearly specify the employment type.

2. Compensation and Benefits
Compensation clauses should detail: base salary, bonuses, commission structure, benefits, and payment schedule. Ambiguity in compensation terms leads to disputes.

3. Working Hours and Overtime
Specify standard working hours and overtime policies. Unlimited overtime clauses without additional compensation may violate labor laws.

4. Leave and Time Off
Detail vacation days, sick leave, parental leave, and other time off. Ensure compliance with local labor laws regarding minimum leave entitlements.

5. Probation Period
Probation periods allow employers to assess fit before full commitment. Typical probation periods range from 3-6 months with clear evaluation criteria.

6. Job Responsibilities
Clearly define the role, responsibilities, and reporting structure. Vague job descriptions can lead to disputes about scope of work.

7. Performance Reviews
Specify the frequency and process for performance evaluations. Regular reviews help manage expectations and document performance issues.

8. Termination Provisions
Detail grounds for termination, notice periods, and severance obligations. Termination for cause should specify what constitutes cause.

9. Non-Solicitation Clauses
Non-solicitation provisions prevent employees from poaching clients or colleagues after leaving. These should be reasonable in scope and duration.

10. Confidentiality Obligations
Employees must protect confidential information during and after employment. Define what constitutes confidential information and the duration of obligations.

11. Intellectual Property Assignment
Work-for-hire clauses assign IP created during employment to the employer. Ensure exceptions for pre-existing IP and personal projects.

12. Restrictive Covenants
Non-compete and non-solicitation clauses must be reasonable. Courts scrutinize these provisions and may refuse to enforce overly broad restrictions.

13. Dispute Resolution
Specify whether employment disputes will be resolved through arbitration or litigation. Some jurisdictions limit mandatory arbitration for employment claims.

14. Modification of Terms
Employment terms should only be modified in writing with mutual consent. Unilateral changes to material terms may constitute constructive dismissal.

15. Compliance with Labor Laws
Employment contracts must comply with applicable labor laws, including minimum wage, working hours, discrimination protections, and safety requirements.
